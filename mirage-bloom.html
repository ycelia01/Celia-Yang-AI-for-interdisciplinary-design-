Mirage Bloom

A slow browser game about listening, where sounds grow a saguaro. Some are mirages, and you learn to tell what is real.

How to play

Keep the real. Let the mirages go.

  1. Listen. Hover a bubble, or tap it once on a touch screen. Its source appears on the land below.
  2. Keep it if it is real. Click it, tap again, or press Enter. Real sounds drift a little every time they play.
  3. Let it go if it is a mirage. Right-click, press X, or press and hold. A mirage repeats exactly and carries a faint electronic tell.
  4. Read the shapes. Clouds, stars and crystals are weather and water. Animals and plants are living things. Human-made shapes are people and machines.
  5. Five stages. Weather, wildlife, layers, people, echoes. At the end you plant your saguaro and can send a sound postcard.
What changed in Week 3

From demo to a place worth staying in.

Week 2 proved the loop. Week 3 made the world feel alive and gave every choice a consequence.

Shapes you can read

Weather and water are clouds, stars, drops and crystals. Animals and plants are leaves, owls, moons and cows. People and machines are cars, planes, trucks, feet and speech bubbles. A mirage can wear any shape, so shape tells you the family, never whether it is real.

A bobcat that bites mirages

Keep a mirage by mistake and the bubble sinks, the bobcat runs over and bites it, and it shatters. The bite also hurts one of the distant saguaros: a bite mark, then a broken top, then a dry, cracked trunk. They are never destroyed, but the desert remembers.

Four painted scenes

A dusty, stormy opening, a clear day, a late afternoon that shifts through three versions, and a dawn. They are AI-assisted paintings that reuse my Week 1 drawing, and they crossfade as the stages change. My saguaro takes the scene's shape and wears a pattern for every sound family you keep.

Too little water, then a flood

The opening plays a real-scene flood recording, because 2026 brought a flood to a drought. After years of dryness, southern Arizona logged a record 246 flash-flood warnings in a single monsoon, and the region is still in drought. Your first task is to find out which water is really there.

An ending with a flute

When the saguaro blooms, a flute plays. You plant it, the land changes, and you choose one of three artworks for a sound postcard to download or send.

Sound library

Recorded, not synthesised.

Most sounds are now real recordings. Mirages replay one exact take, and a faint electronic layer underneath gives them away more gently as the stages go on.

GeophonyWind, rain, thunder, sand, water (laps, bubbling geyser, gurgle, deep rumble) and the flood field recording.
BiophonyCactus wren, wolf howl, rattlesnake, pig, horses (gallop, milling, whinny), dog and cow. Dove, woodpecker, owl, insects and hummingbird are still synthesised.
AnthrophonyFootsteps (grass and gravel), finger whistle, someone close by (cough, swallow, apple bite and chew), prop plane (throttle off, landing, fly-by), truck door and windshield wipers. Traffic, voices and music are still synthesised.
Mirage tellsScanner, static, metal hits, low distorted hits and laser pulses, mixed quietly under every fake sound.
FinaleA flute when the saguaro blooms.
Feedback and playtesting

What I listened to, and what I did about it.

No formal playtest has happened yet; five-person testing is the next step. This round answers the feedback I got on the Week 2 demo and my own play sessions while building. I only mark something as built when it is in the game you can play above.

Visual directionThe Week 2 demo was a plain desert. I made nine reference images (a blurry opening, daytime scenes, late afternoon and dawn) and asked for the game to look like them. Built Four painted scenes and a saguaro that takes each scene's shape. The last three images stay as the postcard artworks.
Shapes you can readI set a rule: each sound family gets its own bubble shapes. Built Clouds, stars and drops for weather and water; leaves, owls and moons for living things; cars, planes, trucks and feet for people and machines.
Mirages should cost somethingA wrong keep used to cost nothing, so a mirage did not feel like a trick. Built The bobcat bites the bubble and a distant saguaro is damaged. Planned A wrong keep also wastes water.
The animal should reactIn Week 2 the bobcat was scenery. Built It runs over and bites a kept mirage. Planned Moods for real sounds, noise and calm.
Each level, a different sceneryBuilt The painted scenes change with the stages. Planned Five real Sonoran habitats tied to times of day.
Explaining the gameI wanted players to know the rule before they start. Built A short how-to-play at the start, in English, Chinese and Spanish.
This year's floodI wanted the game to reflect this year's Southwest weather, drought and flood. Built The opening plays a real flood field recording, with a note about the 2026 floods. Planned A water meter with a dry edge and a flood edge.
Sound pollution and wellbeingI researched the evidence (see below). Planned A noise-load meter and a Hush action. Nothing in the game claims to treat stress.
Enemies and a symphonyPlanned Gentle hazards with no health bar, and an Echo stage where you arrange what you kept into a piece.

Built = playable in this version. Planned = in the Week 3 refinement plan, not yet built.

Changes and their effect

How each change improves the experience.

Recorded soundsReal recordings replace many synthesised tones, so the desert sounds like a place. Real sounds vary a little each time; a mirage replays one exact take, which gives careful listeners something to catch.
Bubble shapesPlayers can narrow down a sound by looking, then decide by listening and by checking whether it has a source on the land.
Bobcat biteA mistake becomes something you see and hear, not a score. The consequence teaches that the sound was fake without a pop-up lecture.
Damaged distant saguarosThe desert remembers your mistakes: bite mark, broken top, cracked trunk. They are never destroyed, so the game stays calm.
Painted backdropsThe game sits on top of the paintings, so the look matches my drawings and each stage feels like a new place. The scenes crossfade slowly, not cut.
Saguaro stylesMy saguaro changes shape with each scene, so growth reads visually and not only as a counter.
How to playThe rule (keep the real, let the mirages go) is clear in under a minute, and the opening stays quiet.
Flood recordingThe first sound is true. It sets the theme of real versus apparent water and ties the game to this year.

What the AI did, and what I decided.

Decisions I made myself

  • The idea: a calm game about hallucination, where sound tells you what is real.
  • Which sounds to use, and their grouping into geophony, biophony and anthrophony.
  • Using the real flood field recording as the opening, and the 2026 flood as the theme.
  • The nine reference images, how I labelled them, and which three are for the postcard.
  • The bubble shape rule, so shape shows the family and never whether it is real.
  • The bobcat bite for a kept mirage, and the damage to distant saguaros.
  • Putting the game on top of the painted images instead of redrawing the scenes.
  • No fail state, and a how-to at the start.
  • How my portfolio is organised: one page per week, kept for reference.

The AI proposed and built; I chose the direction, supplied the recordings and drawings, and reviewed each result.
